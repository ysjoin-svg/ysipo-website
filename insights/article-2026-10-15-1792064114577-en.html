Jointly Developed Patent Applications: How Collaborating Parties Can Agree on Rights

YSIPO Team October 15, 2026 ~5 min read

In today’s innovation-driven economy, collaborative research and development (R&D) is increasingly common, especially in technology-intensive industries such as semiconductors, biotechnology, and green energy. When multiple parties jointly develop an invention, determining ownership and rights to the resulting patent application becomes a critical legal and business issue. In Taiwan, as elsewhere, clear agreements on patent rights at the outset can prevent disputes, protect investments, and facilitate commercialization. This article provides practical guidance for businesses and inventors on how to effectively agree on patent rights in joint development projects under Taiwan’s intellectual property framework.

Understanding Default Rules Under Taiwan Patent Law

Taiwan’s Patent Act does not automatically assign ownership of a jointly developed invention to all collaborating parties. Instead, ownership is determined by contribution to the inventive concept. Under Article 35 of the Patent Act, the right to apply for a patent belongs to the inventor or their successor. When two or more persons jointly create an invention, they are considered joint inventors, and the patent right belongs to them jointly unless otherwise agreed.

However, joint ownership does not imply equal rights to exploit the patent. Under Taiwan law, each joint patent owner may independently exploit the patented invention unless there is an agreement to the contrary. This means that one party could license or use the patent without consulting the others, potentially undermining the commercial interests of collaborators. Moreover, any transfer or licensing of the patent by one joint owner requires the consent of the other joint owners, which can create bottlenecks in commercialization.

Therefore, relying solely on statutory default rules is risky. Parties engaged in joint R&D should proactively define their rights through a written agreement to avoid ambiguity and future conflict.

Key Provisions to Include in a Joint Development Agreement

To safeguard interests and ensure smooth collaboration, a well-drafted joint development agreement should address several key aspects of patent rights:

  • Ownership Allocation: Clearly define how ownership of any resulting patent applications will be split. Options include equal sharing, proportional sharing based on contribution or funding, or assigning ownership to one party with licensing rights granted to others. The agreement should specify how inventorship is determined and documented, ideally with input from patent counsel during the R&D process.
  • Licensing Rights: Establish whether each party will receive exclusive, non-exclusive, or territorial licenses to exploit the patent. For example, one party may retain rights to manufacture in Taiwan, while another gains rights for overseas markets. Field-of-use limitations (e.g., restricting use to specific industries or products) can also be included to prevent internal competition.
  • Patent Prosecution and Maintenance: Outline responsibilities for preparing, filing, and prosecuting patent applications, including cost-sharing arrangements. Decide who will control prosecution strategy (e.g., responding to office actions, appealing rejections) and how decisions will be made—by consensus, majority vote, or designated lead party.
  • Confidentiality and Invention Disclosure: Include robust confidentiality obligations to protect trade secrets and patentable know-how shared during collaboration. Require timely written disclosure of potential inventions to enable proper evaluation and patent filing decisions.
  • Term and Termination: Define the duration of the collaboration and what happens to patent rights if the agreement ends early. Address whether licenses survive termination and under what conditions (e.g., payment of ongoing royalties).
  • Dispute Resolution: Specify governing law (typically Taiwan law for local collaborations), jurisdiction, and preferred dispute resolution mechanisms such as mediation or arbitration to avoid costly litigation.

Practical Tips for Business Owners and Inventors

To maximize the effectiveness of joint development agreements in Taiwan:

  • Engage IP counsel early: Involve a patent attorney or agent during the negotiation phase, not after inventions are made. Early involvement helps identify patentable subject matter, structure agreements to support future patenting, and avoid inadvertent public disclosure.
  • Document contributions meticulously: Maintain clear records of each party’s technical input, experiments, data, and meetings. This documentation supports inventorship determinations and ownership claims if disputes arise.
  • Consider future improvements: Address ownership of improvements or modifications to the joint invention. Decide whether improvements belong to the developing party or are subject to the same joint ownership rules.
  • Register licenses where appropriate: While not always required, recording exclusive licenses with the Taiwan Intellectual Property Office (TIPO) can provide added protection against third parties and clarify rights in enforcement actions.
  • Review and update agreements: As projects evolve, revisit the agreement to reflect changes in scope, funding, or strategic direction. Amendments should be made in writing and signed by all parties.

By taking a proactive and structured approach to defining patent rights in collaborative R&D, parties can align incentives, reduce legal risks, and enhance the value of their innovations. In Taiwan’s competitive innovation landscape, a clear IP agreement is not just a legal formality—it is a strategic asset that enables successful joint ventures and technology transfer.
