Trademark Registration Without Use: Cancellation Risk and Evidence Preservation

YSIPO Team October 19, 2026 ~5 min read

Understanding the Risk of Non-Use Cancellation in Taiwan

In Taiwan, trademark rights are not perpetual simply because a mark has been registered. Under Article 63 of the Taiwan Trademark Act, a registered trademark may be subject to cancellation if it has not been used continuously for a period of three years without justifiable reasons. This provision, commonly referred to as the "non-use cancellation" rule, aims to prevent the hoarding of trademarks and ensure that registered marks are actively used in commerce. For business owners and inventors, this means that merely securing a trademark registration is not enough—ongoing use and proper documentation are essential to maintain legal protection.

The three-year period is calculated from the date of registration, and any interested party—such as a competitor—can file a petition with the Taiwan Intellectual Property Office (TIPO) to request cancellation based on non-use. Once such a petition is filed, the burden of proof shifts to the trademark owner to demonstrate either actual use of the mark during the relevant period or a legitimate reason for non-use (such as import restrictions, government regulations, or force majeure). Failure to provide sufficient evidence may result in the trademark being revoked, leaving the owner without legal recourse against infringers.

Key Evidence to Preserve for Trademark Use

To defend against a non-use cancellation claim, trademark owners must maintain clear, credible, and timely evidence of use. Acceptable forms of evidence in Taiwan include invoices, sales contracts, advertising materials (such as brochures, website screenshots, social media posts, and online advertisements), product packaging, labels, and photographs showing the trademark affixed to goods or used in connection with services. For service-based businesses, evidence may include service agreements, promotional materials, or invoices bearing the trademark.

It is crucial that the evidence clearly displays the trademark as registered—meaning it should match the exact wording, design, or stylization of the registered mark, without significant alterations. Use of a modified version may not be considered valid unless the changes are insignificant and do not alter the distinctive character of the mark. Additionally, the evidence must be dated and preferably originate from within the three-year period in question. Businesses should implement a systematic approach to collecting and storing such documentation, ideally through a dedicated IP management system or regular internal audits.

Practical Steps to Protect Your Trademark Rights

To mitigate the risk of cancellation, businesses should adopt proactive trademark management practices. First, monitor the use of each registered trademark across all goods and services listed in the registration. If a mark is only used for some of the designated items, consider filing for partial cancellation or narrowing the scope of registration to align with actual use—this can strengthen defensibility. Second, maintain a centralized archive of all trademark-related evidence, organized by mark, date, and jurisdiction. Digital backups and timestamped records (such as those from certified email or blockchain-based systems) can enhance credibility.

Third, if genuine obstacles prevent use—such as regulatory delays or supply chain disruptions—document these circumstances thoroughly and be prepared to explain them if challenged. Finally, consider filing a Declaration of Use or submitting evidence of use proactively during renewal periods, even though Taiwan does not currently require such declarations for maintenance. Demonstrating consistent use not only deters cancellation attempts but also reinforces the trademark’s strength in the marketplace.
